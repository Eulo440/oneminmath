Times tables practice

The times tables are the multiplication facts from 2 through 12. Learning one table at a time, then mixing them, is the most reliable path. One Minute Math lets you open any table with no clock, then try a one-minute drill when it feels solid.

Tips that help a table stick

  • 2s, 5s and 10s first. They follow counting patterns most kids already know.
  • 4s are double the 2s. If 6 × 2 is 12, 6 × 4 is 24.
  • 9s have a pattern. Up to 9 × 10, the digits of the product add up to 9 (9 × 4 = 36, 3 + 6 = 9).
  • Turn them around. 8 × 3 is the same as 3 × 8, so each new table adds fewer brand-new facts.

Questions

How do I practice only the 7 times table?

Use the 7s link on this page. It opens practice mode for that table only, from 7 × 1 through 7 × 12.

Should kids time themselves from the start?

Learn a table first with no clock. Once it feels steady, try a one-minute round on that table or mix it with the others.

What comes after the times tables?

Division facts are the same facts backwards. After that, multiplying fractions and decimals uses the tables constantly.
