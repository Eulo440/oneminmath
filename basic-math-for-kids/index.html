Basic math for kids

Basic math is the four operations kids meet first: addition, subtraction, multiplication and division. When those facts are quick, working memory is free for word problems, fractions and everything that comes next.

One Minute Math is a free place to practice them, one problem at a time. There are no ads, no login and no prizes to chase. A minute or two a day is enough to build fluency.

Start at Level 1, additionEasy practice, no clock

What basic math covers

  • Addition: from facts through 10 + 10 up to two-digit addition.
  • Subtraction: minus facts within 20, then within 100 and 200.
  • Multiplication and times tables: the facts from 1 × 1 to 12 × 12.
  • Division: the same facts backwards, up to 144 ÷ 12.

Those are the whole-number facts. Later on, the same site has fraction practice and decimal practice.

One minute a day builds fluency

Fluency means facts like 8 + 6 or 7 × 4 come to mind without counting on fingers. Short, frequent practice does that better than a long session once in a while. Two or three one-minute rounds, most days, is a solid habit. Kids try to beat their own best score, not someone else's.

How to start at Level 1

Level 1 is the easy starting point in every operation. A simple path looks like this:

  1. Addition, Level 1 — facts through 10 + 10.
  2. Subtraction, Level 1 — facts within 20.
  3. Multiplication, Level 1 — tables 1 through 5.
  4. Division, Level 1 — dividing by 1 through 5.
  5. Mixed, Level 1 — all four, still at the easy level.

If the timer feels stressful, switch to practice mode and add the clock later. The grade-by-grade guide suggests levels for grades 1 through 6. Teachers and parents can also read the classroom and home notes.

Questions

What counts as basic math for kids?

The four operations: addition, subtraction, multiplication and division, starting with the easy facts and building up. Fractions and decimals come after the whole-number facts are steady.

Where should my child start?

Addition at Level 1, facts through 10 + 10. Use practice mode first if a timer feels like too much, then try a one-minute round.

Is One Minute Math okay for school and home?

Yes. There are no ads, no login, no prizes and no student accounts. Progress stays on the device.
